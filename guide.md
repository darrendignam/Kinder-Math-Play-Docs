# User guide

Kinder Math has two sides: the **children's side**, where they pick their animal and do
worksheets, and the **grown-ups' area**, where you make worksheets, see results and
change settings.

## Getting started

The first time the app opens, your child picks an animal and types their name. Kinder
Math then makes their first worksheet, and they can start straight away.

After that, the app opens on **Who is playing?**: each child taps their own animal.
More children can be added in the grown-ups' area.

## Doing a worksheet

<figure>
  <img class="screen" loading="lazy" src="{{ '/assets/screens/sheet-list.webp' | relative_url }}" width="1280" height="800" alt="A child's list of worksheets, marked New, Keep going or Done">
  <figcaption>A child's sheets</figcaption>
</figure>

Each child sees their own sheets. A sheet is marked <span class="ui">✨ New</span>,
<span class="ui">✏️ Keep going</span> (started but not finished) or
<span class="ui">⭐ Done</span>. Finished sheets can be done again.
<span class="ui">🎲 Make me a new one!</span> rolls a fresh sheet, if a grown-up has
allowed it in [Settings](#settings).

On a sheet:

- **Count the pictures.** Each number is shown as pictures in ten-frames, so a child can
  count them or see the number at a glance.
- **Answer.** Tap the answer box and pick a number on the number pad, then
  <span class="ui">OK</span>. Depending on [Settings](#settings), the answer is written
  by hand instead.
- **Write anywhere.** Pick a pen colour and draw with a finger or stylus: the name and
  date at the top, tally marks in the dot grid next to each answer, anything at all.
  The toolbar has an eraser, undo, and a bin that clears the drawing (it asks
  "Sure?" first). The ✋ hand moves the page without drawing on it.
- **Landscape.** Turn the tablet sideways for bigger questions. The big arrows move up
  and down one question at a time, and the slider zooms in and out.
- **Finish.** Tap the green tick (<span class="ui">I'm done!</span>). Depending on
  Settings, your child sees how many they got right.

## The grown-ups' area

Tap <span class="ui">🔒 Grown-ups</span> on the **Who is playing?** screen. To keep
young children out, the app asks a times-table question, such as "what is 6 × 7?". Type
the answer on the number pad. There is no PIN to forget.

The area has five tabs: **Worksheets**, **Results**, **Pictures**, **Children** and
**Settings**. <span class="ui">🔒 Back to kids</span> returns to the children's side.

## Making worksheets

<figure>
  <img class="screen" loading="lazy" src="{{ '/assets/screens/roll.webp' | relative_url }}" width="1280" height="800" alt="The Roll a worksheet dialog with sums, difficulty and missing number choices">
  <figcaption>Rolling a worksheet</figcaption>
</figure>

Every worksheet has five questions. The quickest way to make one:

1. On **Worksheets**, tap <span class="ui">＋ New worksheet</span>.
2. In **Roll a worksheet**, choose the **Sums** (adding, taking away, times, sharing),
   the **Difficulty**, and **Which number is missing?**: the answer at the end
   (3 + 2 = _), the middle number (3 + _ = 5), or a mix.
3. Tap <span class="ui">Roll it!</span>.

This is only a starting point. In the editor you can:

- change any number or sign with the − and + buttons, or roll new numbers for the
  whole sheet
- choose for each row whether the **answer** or the **middle** number is hidden
- tick **Bigger numbers** to allow answers up to 100
- choose the **Pictures** shown on the sheet
- rename it, or tap <span class="ui">💡 Suggest</span> for a name like "Adding (Easy)"
- put it in a **Folder**

The app works out every answer, and keeps sums sensible for young children: nothing
goes below zero, and sharing always comes out even. If a row breaks a rule, the editor
says what is wrong before you save.

**Folders** keep sheets tidy (for example "Reception" and "Year 1"). Tap
<span class="ui">＋ Folder</span> to add one; the ✏️ and 🗑️ buttons rename or delete
it. Deleting a folder moves its sheets to **Unfiled**. Deleting a worksheet keeps the
children's past results.

## Results

<figure>
  <img class="screen" loading="lazy" src="{{ '/assets/screens/results.webp' | relative_url }}" width="800" height="1280" style="max-width: 22rem" alt="A finished worksheet in Results, with the child's handwriting and ticks">
  <figcaption>Reviewing a finished sheet</figcaption>
</figure>

**Results** lists every sheet each child has started or finished, with the score. Filter
by child, and tap a row to see the sheet exactly as your child left it, writing and
all. A result can be printed or deleted from there.

## Picture sets

<figure>
  <img class="screen" loading="lazy" src="{{ '/assets/screens/picture-sets.webp' | relative_url }}" width="1280" height="800" alt="The Pictures tab with picture sets and a picture for each number">
  <figcaption>Picture sets</figcaption>
</figure>

Picture sets swap the plain squares on a sheet for pictures to count. Kinder Math comes
with **Balloons** and **Animals** for 1 to 20. You can also make your own from photos of
toys, building blocks, cars, anything at all:

1. On **Pictures**, tap <span class="ui">＋ New picture set</span> and give it a name.
2. Tap <span class="ui">＋ Add</span> on a number and choose a photo showing that many
   things (a photo of 3 cars for 3). Crop, turn and resize it, then save.

Got one photo with all the pieces in it? Use
<span class="ui">✂️ Cut pictures out of one photo</span>: move the box over each piece
and save it as the next number. Pieces cut at the same size keep their real heights, so
a tower of 5 stays taller than a tower of 2.

<figure>
  <img class="screen" loading="lazy" src="{{ '/assets/screens/cut-pictures.webp' | relative_url }}" width="1280" height="800" alt="Cutting numbered pieces out of one photo">
  <figcaption>Cutting pictures out of one photo</figcaption>
</figure>

Pick a set for a sheet in the editor's **Pictures** menu, or make it the default in
Settings. Numbers without a picture fall back to squares.

Only use photos you took yourself or have permission to use. Your photos stay in the
app and are never shared.

## Children

Add, rename or remove children on **Children**. Each child has a name and an animal.
Removing a child also deletes all of their results and drawings.

## Settings

| Setting | Choices |
| --- | --- |
| **Corrections** | **Off**: nobody sees ticks. **Parents only**: ticks show in Results. **Child too**: ticks show when they finish. **As they go**: answer boxes turn green or red after each answer. |
| **Answer boxes** | **Number pad**: tap the box and pick numbers (can be checked). **Digital paper**: write answers with a finger (not checked). |
| **Pictures** | The default picture set for new sheets, and for sheets children roll themselves. |
| **"Make me a new one!" button** | Whether children can roll their own worksheets. |
| **Language** | 17 languages. Each tablet keeps its own choice. |

**About & credits** shows who made the app and the fonts and tools it uses.

## Printing and PDFs

Tap <span class="ui">🖨️ Print / PDF</span> on a worksheet in the editor, or on a result.
The sheet is laid out on A4, with spaces for a name, the date, parent notes and five
stars to colour in.

<figure>
  <img class="screen" loading="lazy" src="{{ '/assets/screens/print.webp' | relative_url }}" width="800" height="1280" style="max-width: 22rem" alt="A worksheet laid out on an A4 page, ready to print">
  <figcaption>A sheet ready to print</figcaption>
</figure>

- **Android:** <span class="ui">🖨️ Print</span> opens Android's print screen. Choose a
  printer, or **Save as PDF**.
- **iPad:** <span class="ui">🖨️ Print</span> uses AirPrint;
  <span class="ui">📄 Share PDF</span> saves to Files or sends it by AirDrop, Mail and
  so on.

## Your data

Everything (children, worksheets, results, drawings, photos and settings) is stored only
on the tablet, inside the app. There is no account and nothing is sent anywhere.

- Each tablet has its own children and sheets. They do not sync between devices.
- Deleting the app deletes all of it.
- Your device's own backup (Google or iCloud) may include the app's data.

See the [privacy policy]({{ '/privacy/' | relative_url }}) for the details.
